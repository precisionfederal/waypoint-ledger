## The GPCI arithmetic, by hand

Twenty of the 5,668 locality figures, drawn at random (seed 20260909) and recomputed by an
independent reader that shares no code with `build_state_prices.py` or `verify_price_table.py`:
it finds the HCPCS line itself, takes the RVUs and the GPCIs out of the two CSVs, and multiplies.
Reproduce with `python3 data/handcheck_gpci_2026_09_09.py <dir holding the two CSVs>`.

Formula, as CMS defines the payment:
`(work RVU × PW GPCI + non-facility PE RVU × PE GPCI + MP RVU × MP GPCI) × 33.4009`

Worked in full, the first three:

```
 1. cms-99212  PR-20 PUERTO RICO   99212 status=A "Office o/p est sf 10 min"
    work 0.70 · non-facility PE 1.02 · MP 0.06   GPCIs PW 1.000 · PE 1.011 · MP 0.985
    (0.70×1.000 + 1.02×1.011 + 0.06×0.985) × 33.4009 = 59.7983 → $59.80   table says $59.80

 2. cms-99204  WV-16 WEST VIRGINIA 99204 status=A "Office o/p new mod 45 min"
    work 2.60 · non-facility PE 2.47 · MP 0.24   GPCIs PW 1.000 · PE 0.869 · MP 1.431
    (2.60×1.000 + 2.47×0.869 + 0.24×1.431) × 33.4009 = 170.0062 → $170.01  table says $170.01

 3. cms-img-mri-brain-nc CA-71 EL CENTRO 70551 status=A "Mri brain stem w/o dye"
    work 1.44 · non-facility PE 4.31 · MP 0.10   GPCIs PW 1.017 · PE 1.096 · MP 0.541
    (1.44×1.017 + 4.31×1.096 + 0.10×0.541) × 33.4009 = 208.4998 → $208.50  table says $208.50
```

## What was wrong, and what happened to it

### FIXED — the trips card, and the check behind it

`verify_burdens.py` failed: it asserted that no row in the table may name travel at all, and the
table had since gained the GSA privately-owned-vehicle rate ($0.76 a mile, effective 1 July 2026,
non-summable, labelled INPUT ONLY). The assertion was wrong, not the table — a per-mile
reimbursement rate for federal travellers is not a price for a trip to care. But the trips card was
also telling people "No published federal figure in this price table prices a trip to care", which
read as *the table holds nothing*, while the table held that rate.

Both ends were fixed. The check is now two-sided: nothing that names travel may be **summable** (a
per-trip figure could otherwise enter a medical total), **and** where the table holds a
non-summable per-mile input, the trips card must name it, so nothing is hidden from the person.
The card now names it, still prices nothing, and still adds nothing.
`verify_burdens.py`: **19 PASS · 0 FAIL.**

### FIXED — `data/unpriceable.json` had grown stale in four places

This file is rendered to the person on the ledger ("What would fix it: …"), so a stale sentence is a
false statement on screen, not a stale note.

1. **`out-of-pocket-share`** cited *MEPS Statistical Brief #549*, which no row in the table cites any
   more, and said no published split exists. One does now, and it is a finding worth having: the
   national estimate that tested it found **no significant difference** — $236 a year, interval
   −$95 to +$566, p = 0.162. The entry now says that, and still refuses to put a number on the line.
2. **`caregiver-time`** said "Until the tool does that, this line stays blank." The tool does it: four
   published BLS rates, $17.21 to $46.90 an hour, each with its arithmetic shown.
3. **`lost-work`** described its fix in the future tense. The workdays card does it now — and the
   entry now says plainly that the card *opens* on the published national median with the division
   on its face, which is what actually happens.
4. **`travel`** pointed only at the IRS medical mileage rate. The table carries the GSA rate; the
   entry now names what the table actually holds, with what it is and is not.

### FIXED — `data/invisible-events.json` contradicted itself, and overstated MEPS

The README claimed "Every event listed here produces ZERO ROWS in MEPS, HCUP and CMS claims," while
two of its own six categories say the opposite in their own text: a dismissal and care aimed at the
wrong target are **billed**, and appear in the data as ordinary delivered care. And MEPS is not only
its expenditure event files — the household survey asks separately whether a person was unable to
get care or delayed it, and those access-to-care items ride on the Full Year Consolidated file
(AHRQ, MEPS HC-243, 2022, verified at meps.ahrq.gov during this audit).

The README now says the precise thing: no federal **expenditure** file carries a dollar or a row for
any of these; two of the six are billed and still invisible, which is worse; and MEPS's national
count of unmet need exists, carries no dollar figure, and is not an account of one person's search.
That is a stronger claim to defend in front of a health economist than the blanket one it replaced.

### NOT FIXED — the BLS weekly-earnings row says the tool asks first, and the tool does not

`bls2026q2-median-weekly-earnings` opens with:

> 🔴 THIS IS AN INPUT, NOT A PRICE, AND THIS TOOL WILL NOT APPLY IT TO YOU WITHOUT ASKING. It is
> offered so you can see what a national median looks like; the lost-work line in this ledger is
> priced from YOUR OWN pay, which you supply, or it stays blank.

`lib/burdens.ts` defaults `payBasis` to `'median'`, so entering days immediately prices them at
$250.20 a day from that row. The line is never added to the medical total, and the card shows the
division and the basis picker, so nothing is concealed — but the sentence as written is not what the
product does.

**Not fixed in this lane** because `data/prices.json` is shared with the catalog lane tonight and
editing it also requires regenerating `public/data.json`, `public/data/price-table.json` and
`public/data/dictionary.json`, all three of which already carry another session's uncommitted
changes. It is a one-sentence change; the replacement is in the lane report.

### UNVERIFIED (1) — a live URL that no longer shows the wage

`bls2025-caregiver-replacement-wages` cites `https://www.bls.gov/oes/current/oes_nat.htm`. That page
answers 200 but now serves the OEWS Tables landing page, with no occupational figure on it. The
figures themselves are correct: they were re-read tonight in the published data file for the same
release (`oesm25nat/national_M2025_dl.xlsx`, from `https://www.bls.gov/oes/special-requests/oesm25nat.zip`)
— 31-1120 median hourly $17.21, 31-1131 $20.32, 29-1141 $46.90, 00-0000 $24.51, and 4,305,810 aides
employed. So the number is verified and the *link a reader would click* is not. Repointing
`source_url` at the data file is a `data/prices.json` edit, held for the same reason as above.
